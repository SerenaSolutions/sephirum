\begin{abstract}
We present ZEPHIRUM, a system that answers questions \emph{about} computations
before running them. Problems are stated in ZEPHIRUM, a small language in which
the user declares a question (e.g., ``is the sum above a threshold?''), a
contract (tolerance, budget, assumptions) and a model. The Zephirum Compilation
Algorithm (ZCA) searches an \emph{elimination ladder} of increasingly expensive
techniques --- identity, simplification, reduction, interval bounding,
closed-form evaluation, certified approximation, classical execution --- and
returns one of five explicit states: decided without execution, decided by
reduction, residual computation required, full execution required, or UNKNOWN.
Every decision is emitted with a certificate that an independent verifier
re-derives from the original source. We introduce the numeral Z (zephirum), the
third truth value of ZEPHIRUM logic, which remains whenever neither necessity
nor unnecessity can be proven. On 500{,}000 randomized problems across four
families, the system made zero wrong decisions, all 500{,}000 certificates
passed independent verification, 20/20 forged certificates were rejected, and
76.5\% of the demanded computation units were eliminated by certificate. The
decision layer has been re-implemented and cross-checked in independent C, the
same source transpiles to autonomous Python, C, Java and C\# programs with
identical verdicts and certificates, and the elimination kernels of three
families are self-hosted in the language itself, executed by a budgeted
virtual machine. We claim no quantum capability; the architecture is
deliberately quantum-last.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Computation exists to answer questions, yet the standard pipeline --- pick an
algorithm, run it, then interpret --- pays for execution before asking whether
execution is needed. ZEPHIRUM inverts this order: PROOF $\rightarrow$ COMPUTE.
Given a problem $P$, a question $Q$ and a contract $C$ (tolerance $\epsilon$,
budget $B$, model $M$), the compiler searches for a \emph{decision kernel}: a
certified procedure sufficient to decide $Q$ under $C$ at minimal justified
cost. The design principle is stated in the project charter: the generator may
be heuristic or AI-based; the verifier must be independent.

\section{Prior art (recorded honestly)}
The system composes known techniques. Partial evaluation and program
specialization justify eliminating unreachable work. Interval arithmetic
supplies the bounding rungs. Proof-carrying code and certified computation
supply the certificate/verifier separation. Cost models in query optimization
predate the idea of deciding before executing. This report claims a
\emph{composition} --- an elimination ladder with cost-ordered rungs, a
trivalent decision logic with an explicit UNKNOWN state, and a composite
certificate with ledger --- not the invention of any constituent technique. An
exhaustive equivalence search (e.g., against quantum resource-estimation
toolkits) remains future work and is recorded as an open verification in the
charter.

\section{Architecture}
\textbf{ZEPHIRUM} (from Arabic \emph{\c{s}ifr}, the etymological root of
``zero'') declares ASK (the question), CONTRACT (tolerance, error model), and
MODEL (computation family, assumptions, unknowns with optional bounds). The
ZCA ladder is ordered by cost: rung attempts are cheap first; the first
certified success decides the question and emits a certificate; exhaustion of
the ladder justifies execution. Five states are explicit; in particular,
UNKNOWN is a first-class result, never a failure.

\textbf{The numeral Z (zephirum).} ZEPHIRUM logic is trivalent: 0
(false/eliminated), 1 (true/necessary), and Z --- the question not yet
collapsed. The decision kernel is the collapse operator $Z \to 0|1$, always
accompanied by a verifiable certificate. In quantum notation the concept is
named by the plus state $Z \equiv (|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$; we stress
that this is a \emph{naming}, not new mathematics, and the current
implementation is classical. The name ZEPHIRUM comes from the medieval Latin
\emph{zephirum} --- the form in which Fibonacci recorded the numeral in the
\emph{Liber Abaci} (1202), the step that became ``zero'' in Europe.

\textbf{Certificates.} Each certificate carries the deciding rung, its
evidence (witness sums, bounds, evaluated values), and the eliminated units.
The verifier re-derives every number from the original source, using a method
different from the engine's where possible, and rejects any inconsistency.

\section{Experiments}
All experiments are reproducible from the reference repository with a fixed
seed (stdlib-only Python); an executable conformance suite of ten batteries
defines the current standard (v0.3) and must pass in full for any
implementation to claim the name.

\textbf{Falsification battery.} 500{,}000 randomized problems in four
families (threshold sums with declared monotonicity; sums with an unknown under
optional bounds with an oracle; unknowns without oracle where UNKNOWN is the
only correct outcome; partial means with five unknowns, bounds present or
absent). Ground truth was computed by brute force independently of the engine.
Results: zero wrong answers in 468{,}582 decisions; 500{,}000/500{,}000
certificates verified; 31{,}418 honest UNKNOWNs (6.3\%); 76.5\% of 3{,}714{,}285
demanded units eliminated by certificate.

\textbf{Soundness attack.} Twenty valid certificates were tampered with
(witness sums, base sums, bounds, evaluated values incremented). The
independent verifier rejected 20/20.

\textbf{Advisory layer.} A Naive Bayes classifier over cheap metadata
(family, size, threshold ratio) trained on 40{,}000 ledger entries predicts the
final state before analysis: 95.67\% accuracy on 10{,}000 held-out cases
(majority baseline 53.05\%); recall 100\% for full-execution outcomes, 90\% for
UNKNOWN, 0\% for the rare residual class (0.5\% of corpus) --- a recorded
limitation. The layer is advisory by construction: predictions reorder
attention but never produce decisions or certificates.

\textbf{Cross-language verification.} The reference verifier has been
re-implemented in pure C11 (independent SHA-256, \_\_int128 cross-multiplied
exact comparisons). Across 520 certificate cases spanning four bootstrapped
families (Gauss sums, infinite geometric series, arithmetic means, finite
geometric series), the C verifier agrees with the Python reference on
520/520 verdicts, input hashes and normative unit costs, and rejects 8/8
deliberately forged certificates (flipped verdicts, swapped sources, broken
budgets, identity degeneracies presented as legitimate).

\textbf{Multi-target transpilation.} A single ZEPHIRUM source transpiles to
autonomous programs in Python, C, Java and C\#: identical verdict, identical
input hash, identical certified units on 90/90 sources; the C target is
executed natively (gcc), the Java and C\# targets are generated with declared
verification status.

\textbf{Self-hosting.} Three elimination kernels are written in the language
itself and executed by a budgeted virtual machine in which the certificate
authorizes a hard number of data units: the Gauss sum $n(n+1)/2$ (2 units),
the infinite geometric series $a/(1-r)$ (2 units), the arithmetic mean
$(n+1)/2$ (1 unit), and --- with stack opcodes DUP/SWAP/POW added to the ISA
--- the finite geometric series $(r^{n+1}-1)/(r-1)$ (2 units, independently
cross-checked against a naive summation on 120 randomized cases plus the C
verifier). A forged program cannot spend beyond its certificate: budget,
step, call-depth and exponent walls stop it with explicit faults.

\textbf{Q-SIM Gateway.} A first product layer routes entanglement-family
questions in front of quantum SDKs (Qiskit, Cirq, PennyLane): the question is
decided analytically at the gate with zero billed QPU units and a certificate;
only the residual is routed. Where all three SDKs break by
catastrophic cancellation (the $2^{53}$ precision frontier), the exact
certificate is the only stable verdict --- measured, not claimed.

\textbf{Negative results, reported with equal weight.} Cases requiring full
execution and UNKNOWN outcomes carry negative net benefit (analysis cost
unpaid); the aggregate figures include them. A latent generator defect found
during integration was fixed and all published numbers regenerated to match
the fixed code exactly.

\section{Limitations}
The families tested have exploitable algebraic structure by construction; the
76.5\% figure does not generalize to arbitrary domains. The analysis cost is
negligible here but may not remain so when rungs require expensive proofs. No
quantum backend exists; the final rung is future work. Prior-art equivalence
has not been exhaustively established.

\section{Conclusion}
ZEPHIRUM demonstrates, in a controlled but fully reproducible setting, that a
question-first compiler with independent verification can eliminate most
demanded computation while remaining unable to lie: zero wrong answers, all
certificates verified, all forgeries rejected --- in two independent
implementations and four transpiled targets. The deeper claim is
methodological: before asking machines to compute more, ask whether they need
to compute at all.
