\begin{abstract}
We present ZEPHIRUM (ZYQL), a domain-specific language and exact-decision
kernel that answers questions about quantum computations \emph{before} any
execution. A question is stated in ZYQL as
\texttt{ASK/CONTRACT/MODEL}; the kernel decides it with exact rational
arithmetic (the \emph{necessity compiler} paradigm: proof first, execution
only of what remains) and emits a SHA-256 certificate verifiable by any
third party. Verdicts are trivalent: \textbf{1}, \textbf{0}, or
\textbf{UNKNOWN} --- the kernel never guesses. On 500{,}000 randomized
problems the kernel returned zero wrong answers, eliminated 76.5\% of
computations by certificate, and rejected 20/20 forged certificates.
Every hardware experiment reported here was gated: no quantum job was
submitted without a prior offline exact verdict. On three IBM Heron
processors (ibm\_fez, ibm\_marrakesh, ibm\_kingston) the empirical evidence
agreed with the exact decision in both directions (entangled and
separable controls), with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals excluding
zero for Bell correlations and containing zero for separable controls. A
GHZ scaling ladder (4, 6, 8, 12, 20 qubits) shows pole populations decaying
with chain depth (95.1\%, 91.8\%, 72.5\%, 53.5\%, 39.7\%) while end-pair
correlators remain high, consistent with the exact verdicts decided at
zero QPU cost. An overhead audit of the hybrid pipeline shows QPU active
time below 1.6\% of wall time, dominated by queue waits --- the practical
case for deciding before executing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The standard workflow of quantum computing is
\textsc{algorithm}~$\rightarrow$~\textsc{compute}: a circuit is built, run,
and interpreted. ZEPHIRUM inverts the paradigm to
\textsc{proof}~$\rightarrow$~\textsc{compute}: given a question about a
computation, the system first tries to prove that \emph{no computation is
needed}, and only executes what remains, with a verifiable certificate.
The name is the Latin form of \emph{zero} recorded by Fibonacci in
\emph{Liber Abaci} (1202).

Three design commitments:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exactness.} The classical kernel decides with integer and
  rational arithmetic (\texttt{Fraction}); no floating-point tolerance
  enters a verdict.
  \item \textbf{Trivalence.} Verdicts are 1, 0, or UNKNOWN. An honest
  UNKNOWN is recorded, never rounded into a binary answer.
  \item \textbf{Evidence before velocity.} Quantum-hardware runs are
  empirical evidence of physical plausibility, never the certificate
  (\S\ref{sec:honesty}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The ZYQL Language}
A program states a question, a contract, and a model:
\begin{verbatim}
ASK:
    question: entangled == 1
CONTRACT:
    absolute_error: 0
MODEL:
    type: entanglement
    state: 0.7071,0,...,0.7071
\end{verbatim}
The contract fixes the acceptable error (zero for exact questions). The
kernel compiles the question through the \emph{Zephirum Compilation
Algorithm} (ZCA), an elimination ladder that attempts successive exact
reductions; whatever survives the ladder is executed, and a certificate
is emitted either way.

\section{Exact Kernel}
Entanglement questions are decided by the Schmidt determinant criterion on
the exact rational state vector: a bipartition with a rank-2 flattening
of determinant zero implies separability. Decisions are recursive over
bipartitions with rational cross-product tests; complexity is polynomial
in the support size for the sparse families tested. The 20-qubit GHZ
rung was decided from a 2-entry sparse support without materializing
$2^{20}$ amplitudes.

\section{Certificates and Independent Verification}
Every verdict emits a certificate containing the normalized state data
string and its SHA-256 hash. Third parties re-derive the verdict from
the certificate without trusting the issuer. In a forgery battery, 20/20
forged certificates were rejected by independent verifiers. In the
measured battery, 500{,}000/500{,}000 emitted certificates were
independently verified.

\section{Hardware Validation Gating}
\label{sec:honesty}
Policy: \emph{no QPU job without a prior exact ZEPHIRUM verdict.} Every
hardware experiment below was preceded by an offline exact decision
(certificate hash recorded in the receipt), submitted only afterwards,
and billed at zero QPU cost for the decision itself. This inverts the
usual role of the QPU: it does not decide, it witnesses. All jobs ran on
the IBM Quantum free Open Plan (Heron, 156 qubits) with minimal,
evidence-grade usage; every job is listed in the public QPU ledger
(\texttt{results/qpu\_ledger.md}) with job IDs.

\subsection{Positive and Negative Controls}
Bell $\Phi^+$ (exact verdict: entangled) measured
$\langle ZZ\rangle = 0.8857$ and $\langle XX\rangle = 0.9092$
(2$\times$2048 shots, ibm\_fez): correlation in both bases. The
separable control $|+0\rangle$ (exact verdict: not entangled) measured
$\langle ZZ\rangle = 0.0508$, $\langle XX\rangle = -0.0742$: no
correlation. A product state cannot show both correlations near 1; a
Bell state cannot show both near 0. The hardware agrees with the exact
decision in both directions.

\subsection{Cross-Hardware Bootstrap}

before any submission.

\subsection{GHZ Scaling Ladder}
GHZ states of 4, 6, 8, 12 and 20 qubits were emitted by the fusion chain
(.zeph $\rightarrow$ exact verdict $\rightarrow$ OpenQASM 3
$\rightarrow$ QPU) and executed on ibm\_fez and ibm\_marrakesh
(512 shots per rung). All five exact verdicts (entangled = 1) were
decided offline at zero QPU cost.

Pole populations dilute with routed CX-chain depth, as expected from
unmitigated hardware; end-pair correlators remain high, evidencing
long-range GHZ structure through decoherence. For the 20-qubit rung,
72.3\% of shots fall within Hamming distance 2 of the poles. The
physical dilution is recorded honestly; the verdicts were never taken
from the hardware.

\subsection{Cluster-State (QCNN) Signature}
The 1D cluster state cell (CZ$|++\rangle$) of Cong et al.\
(arXiv:1810.03787) was confronted with the kernel (exact verdict:
entangled) and the QPU (3$\times$512 shots, ibm\_fez): stabilizers
$\langle XZ\rangle = 0.918$, $\langle ZX\rangle = 0.844$,
$\langle ZZ\rangle = 0.012$ --- the cluster signature, confirming the
exact decision.

\section{Overhead Audit: Why Deciding First Matters}
Instrumenting the hybrid pipeline on real jobs (ibm\_kingston) gives, per
job: queue wait 4455\,s / 287\,s / 46\,s, QPU active share of wall time
0.044\% / 0.512\% / 1.595\%. Queue time dominates end-to-end latency by
two to three orders of magnitude over useful QPU time. Deciding
questions offline --- at zero QPU cost --- before spending wall-clock
queue time is therefore not an optimization but a categorical
efficiency gain: in the Prova Viva battery, 100/100 decisions were made
offline with zero QPU cost.

\section{Limitations and Honesty}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item QPU runs are empirical evidence, never the certificate
  (\S\ref{sec:honesty}); nothing in this paper claims hardware-verified
  quantum advantage.
  \item The classical kernel is exact for the tested families
  (entanglement/separability, stabilizer families, triangular
  determinant families); larger algebraic families remain future work.
  \item The advisory AI layer is optional and clearly labeled; it never
  overrides an exact verdict.
  \item Sensational counterexamples in the literature (e.g., Kaplansky
  type 2) are treated as unexecuted prescriptions, per their own papers.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
ZEPHIRUM demonstrates a working decide-before-execute discipline for
quantum questions: trivalent verdicts, exact offline certificates,
independent verification, and hardware evidence gated behind prior
decisions --- on free-tier access. All artifacts (language, grammar,
emitters, receipts, ledger, demo) are public.
